Q1. What is Software Testing? Explain its objectives and importance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Software Testing is the process of executing a software application or
system with the intention of finding defects (bugs), and of checking whether
the software meets the specified requirements and works as expected. It
compares the actual behaviour of the software with the expected behaviour.

OBJECTIVES OF SOFTWARE TESTING:
1. To find defects (errors, faults, failures) as early as possible.
2. To verify that the software meets the business and user requirements.
3. To build confidence in the quality of the software.
4. To prevent defects from reaching the end user or production.
5. To ensure the software is reliable, secure, and performs well.
6. To provide the stakeholders with information so they can make decisions
   about the release of the product.
7. To check that the software complies with standards and regulations.

IMPORTANCE OF SOFTWARE TESTING:
1. Cost saving: A defect found early is much cheaper to fix than one found
   after release.
2. Quality: It improves the overall quality and reliability of the product.
3. Security: It reveals vulnerabilities that could be exploited by attackers.
4. Customer satisfaction: A defect-free product gives a better user
   experience and builds trust.
5. Reputation: Failures in production (e.g. in banking or healthcare
   software) can cause financial loss, legal trouble and damage to the
   brand.
6. Safety: In critical systems (aviation, medical, automotive) testing can
   prevent loss of life.

Q2. What is Software Quality Assurance (SQA)? Explain its role in software
    development.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Software Quality Assurance (SQA) is a systematic, process-oriented set of
activities that ensures the software development and maintenance processes
are properly defined, followed, and continuously improved so that the final
product meets the required quality standards. It focuses on preventing
defects rather than detecting them.

ROLE OF SQA IN SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT:
1. Defining and establishing standards, procedures and methodologies for
   development.
2. Ensuring that the defined processes are actually followed by the team.
3. Preventing defects by improving the process (proactive approach).
4. Conducting reviews, walkthroughs, inspections and audits of work
   products.
5. Monitoring quality metrics (defect density, defect removal efficiency,
   etc.).
6. Managing risks related to quality throughout the project.
7. Providing management with visibility into project quality.
8. Ensuring proper documentation and configuration management.
9. Encouraging continuous process improvement.

Q4. Explain the major objectives of Software Quality Assurance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

The major objectives of SQA are:

1. Quality improvement: To improve the quality of the software product by
   improving the process used to develop it.
2. Defect prevention: To prevent defects early in the life cycle instead of
   finding them late.
3. Compliance with standards: To make sure the project follows the
   organisational and international standards (ISO 9001, CMMI, IEEE).
4. Requirement conformance: To ensure that the software meets the specified
   functional and non-functional requirements.
5. Process monitoring: To verify that the defined processes and procedures
   are being followed.
6. Visibility: To give management and stakeholders clear information about
   the quality status of the project.
7. Risk reduction: To identify and reduce quality risks early.
8. Customer satisfaction: To deliver a product that satisfies user needs and
   expectations.
9. Continuous improvement: To collect feedback and metrics and continually
   improve processes.
10. Cost and time control: To reduce rework, and so reduce cost and delays.

Q5. What is the difference between Quality Assurance (QA) and Quality
    Control (QC)?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Quality Assurance (QA) is a process-oriented activity that aims to prevent
defects by making sure the right processes and standards are followed.
Quality Control (QC) is a product-oriented activity that aims to identify
defects in the finished product by inspection and testing.

Basis          | Quality Assurance (QA)         | Quality Control (QC)
---------------|--------------------------------|----------------------------------
Nature         | Process oriented               | Product oriented
Approach       | Preventive                     | Detective / corrective
Objective      | Prevent defects                | Identify and fix defects
Focus          | "Are we building the product   | "Are we building the right
               | the right way?"                | product?" (checking the output)
Timing         | Throughout the SDLC            | After the product (or a part of
               |                                | it) is built
Activities     | Audits, process definition,    | Testing, inspection, reviews of
               | training, standards            | the product
Type           | Proactive                      | Reactive
Responsibility | Whole team / QA team           | Testing team
Example        | Defining coding standards      | Running test cases to find bugs

Q6. Explain the role and responsibilities of a Software Quality Assurance
    Engineer.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

A Software Quality Assurance Engineer is responsible for making sure that
the software development process and the final product meet the defined
quality standards. He/she works with developers, testers, business analysts
and managers throughout the SDLC.

ROLE AND RESPONSIBILITIES:
1. Understand the requirements and review them for clarity, completeness and
   testability.
2. Prepare the SQA plan, test strategy and test plans.
3. Define, implement and improve quality standards, processes and
   procedures.
4. Design test cases and test scenarios, and prepare test data.
5. Execute manual and automated tests and report defects.
6. Track defects until closure, and verify fixes (re-testing and regression
   testing).
7. Conduct reviews, walkthroughs, inspections and process audits.
8. Collect and analyse quality metrics (defect density, test coverage,
   defect leakage) and prepare reports.
9. Perform root cause analysis of defects and suggest corrective and
   preventive actions.
10. Ensure documentation is complete and maintained.
11. Train team members on quality practices.
12. Communicate quality status and risks to stakeholders.
13. Take part in release decisions (go / no-go).
14. Promote continuous improvement.

SKILLS REQUIRED: Analytical thinking, knowledge of SDLC and testing
techniques, familiarity with tools (Selenium, JIRA, Postman, etc.),
communication and attention to detail.

Q7. Explain the relationship between Software Testing, Quality Assurance,
    and Quality Control.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Quality Assurance, Quality Control and Testing are related, and together
they achieve software quality, but they work at different levels.

- Quality Assurance (QA) is the broadest concept. It covers the whole
  process: standards, methods, audits, reviews, training. It is preventive.
- Quality Control (QC) is a subset of QA. It is a set of activities
  (inspection, reviews, testing) to check that the product meets the quality
  requirements. It is detective.
- Software Testing is a subset of QC. It is the actual execution of the
  software to find defects.

Hierarchy:

        +--------------------------------------------+
        |  Quality Assurance (Process - Prevention)  |
        |   +-------------------------------------+  |
        |   | Quality Control (Product - Detection)|  |
        |   |   +-----------------------------+   |  |
        |   |   |      Software Testing       |   |  |
        |   |   +-----------------------------+   |  |
        |   +-------------------------------------+  |
        +--------------------------------------------+

In short: QA makes sure we are following the right process (prevent), QC
checks that the product is correct (detect), and Testing is the main
technique used by QC to find bugs. The findings from testing and QC are fed
back to QA to improve the process, which forms a cycle of improvement.

10. Risk management: Identifying and reducing quality risks.
11. Training: Educating the team on standards and tools.
12. Reporting: Giving reports to management on quality status.
13. Process improvement: Recommending changes based on feedback and
    metrics.

Q9. Explain how Software Testing helps in improving software quality.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Software testing improves quality in the following ways:

1. Detects defects early: Bugs are found and fixed before release, so the
   product is more stable.
2. Ensures requirement conformance: Testing verifies that every requirement
   is implemented correctly.
3. Improves reliability: Repeated testing under different conditions
   reduces failures in production.
4. Improves performance: Performance and load testing find bottlenecks
   and make the software fast and scalable.
5. Improves security: Security testing finds vulnerabilities and protects
   user data.
6. Improves usability: Usability testing ensures the software is easy to
   use.
7. Ensures compatibility: The software is checked on various devices,
   browsers and operating systems.
8. Reduces cost: Fixing defects early is cheaper than post-release fixes.
9. Builds confidence: Test results give stakeholders confidence to release.
10. Provides measurable data: Test metrics show the state of the product
    and help in decisions.
11. Prevents regression: Regression testing ensures new changes do not break
    existing features.

Q10. What is a Quality Management System (QMS)? Explain its importance in
     software development.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

A Quality Management System (QMS) is a formalised system that documents the
processes, procedures, responsibilities and resources needed to achieve
quality objectives and to consistently meet customer requirements. In
software, it defines how software is planned, developed, tested, released
and maintained with quality in mind. ISO 9001 is a well-known QMS standard.

COMPONENTS OF A QMS:
- Quality policy and quality objectives
- Documented processes and procedures
- Roles and responsibilities
- Resources and training
- Monitoring, measurement and audits
- Corrective and preventive actions
- Continual improvement

IMPORTANCE OF QMS IN SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT:
1. Provides a consistent and standard way of working across projects.
2. Improves product quality and reduces defects.
3. Increases customer satisfaction and trust.
4. Reduces rework, cost and delivery time.
5. Improves the efficiency and productivity of teams.
6. Helps meet legal, regulatory and contractual requirements.
7. Provides better documentation and traceability.
8. Supports risk management and decision-making based on data.
9. Provides a framework for continuous improvement.
10. Gives competitive advantage and helps in certification (ISO, CMMI).

Q11. Explain the importance of standards, processes, and documentation in
     Software Quality Assurance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

STANDARDS
Standards (e.g. ISO 9001, ISO/IEC 25010, IEEE 829, CMMI, coding standards)
are agreed rules and guidelines for building software.
- They ensure consistency and uniformity across the project.
- They provide a benchmark against which quality can be measured.
- They make the software easier to maintain and understand.
- They help in complying with legal and customer requirements.
- They allow easier communication and integration between teams.

PROCESSES
A process is a defined set of steps to carry out development and testing
activities (e.g. requirement analysis process, review process, change
management, defect management).
- They make the work repeatable and predictable.
- They reduce dependence on individuals.
- They help in preventing defects.
- They make it possible to measure and improve performance.
- They ensure everyone follows the same best practices.

DOCUMENTATION
Documentation includes SRS, design documents, test plans, test cases, user
manuals, defect reports and review records.
- It provides a clear record of requirements, decisions and results.
- It supports traceability from requirements to test cases.
- It helps new team members understand the system.
- It is evidence for audits and reviews.
- It makes maintenance and future enhancements easier.
- It improves communication between stakeholders.

Together, standards define WHAT is expected, processes define HOW to do it,
and documentation records EVIDENCE that it was done.

Q12. What is a Software Quality Assurance Plan (SQAP)? Explain its major
     components.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

A Software Quality Assurance Plan (SQAP) is a formal document that describes
the quality activities, standards, procedures, tools, responsibilities and
schedule that will be used to ensure a software project meets its quality
goals. It is usually prepared at the start of the project (IEEE 730 provides
a standard format).

MAJOR COMPONENTS OF AN SQAP:
1. Purpose and Scope: Aim of the plan and the products and activities it
   covers.
2. Reference Documents: Standards and documents referred to (SRS, IEEE
   standards, etc.).
3. Management: Organisation structure, roles, responsibilities and tasks of
   the quality team.
4. Documentation: List of documents to be produced and maintained (SRS,
   design, test plan, user manual).
5. Standards, Practices and Conventions: Coding, naming, design and
   testing standards to be followed.
6. Reviews and Audits: Types of reviews (walkthroughs, inspections) and
   audits, and their schedule.
7. Testing Strategy: Test levels, types, tools, entry and exit criteria.
8. Problem Reporting and Corrective Action: How defects are logged,
   tracked, and resolved.
9. Tools, Techniques and Methodologies: Tools used for testing, defect
   tracking and configuration management.
10. Configuration / Change Management: Version control and change control
    procedures.
11. Metrics: Quality metrics to be collected (defect density, test coverage).
12. Risk Management: Identified quality risks and mitigation.
13. Training: Training needs for the project team.
14. Records Collection, Maintenance and Retention: How quality records are
    stored.

Q13. Explain the difference between Verification and Validation and discuss
     their role in software quality.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

VERIFICATION: The process of evaluating work products (requirements, design,
code, documents) to check whether they meet the specified requirements at
each development stage. It answers: "Are we building the product RIGHT?"

VALIDATION: The process of evaluating the final software to check that it
meets the actual needs and expectations of the user. It answers: "Are we
building the RIGHT product?"

Basis         | Verification                    | Validation
--------------|---------------------------------|--------------------------------
Meaning       | Checks if the product conforms  | Checks if the product meets user
              | to specifications               | needs
Question      | Are we building the product     | Are we building the right
              | right?                          | product?
Type          | Static testing (no code         | Dynamic testing (code is
              |  execution)                     | executed)
Activities    | Reviews, walkthroughs,          | Functional testing, system
              | inspections, desk checking      | testing, UAT
Performed on  | Documents, design, code         | Actual working software
Timing        | Early phases, before execution  | After code is ready
Done by       | QA team, developers, peers      | Testers, end users, customers
Error type    | Finds errors in documents and   | Finds errors in the working
              | design                          | product

ROLE IN SOFTWARE QUALITY:
- Verification finds defects early (in requirements and design) where fixing
  costs less.
- Validation ensures the final product actually satisfies the customer.
- Together they cover the whole SDLC (as in the V-Model) and give complete
  assurance that the product is both built correctly and is the correct
  product.

Q14. Explain how defects can affect the overall quality of software.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

A defect (bug) is a flaw in the software that causes it to behave
incorrectly or differently from the expected behaviour. Defects affect
quality in many ways:

1. Functionality: Features may not work correctly, or may be missing.
2. Reliability: Frequent crashes and failures reduce trust in the software.
3. Performance: Defects such as memory leaks and inefficient logic slow
   down the system.
4. Security: Defects such as SQL injection or weak authentication can lead
   to data theft and breaches.
5. Usability: Bugs in the interface or workflow frustrate users.
6. Maintainability: Defective, tangled code is difficult to change and
   leads to more defects.
7. Cost: Defects found late are costly to fix (rework, patches, support).
8. Schedule: Fixing defects delays release dates.
9. Customer satisfaction: Users become dissatisfied and may switch to
   competitors.
10. Reputation and legal impact: Serious failures can cause financial loss,
    penalties and loss of brand image.
11. Safety: In critical systems, defects can even endanger human life.

Severity of the impact depends on the type of defect (critical, major,
minor, trivial) and where it occurs.

Q15 and Q16. A banking application frequently crashes during online
             transactions. As a QA professional, explain what activities
             you would perform to improve the quality of the application.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Note: In the assignment sheet this question is split over numbers 15 and
16, so it is answered together.)

ANSWER:

As a QA professional I would follow a structured approach:

1. UNDERSTAND AND REPRODUCE THE PROBLEM
   - Collect crash reports, error logs, screenshots and user complaints.
   - Find out when it happens (peak hours, specific transaction types,
     particular devices or browsers).
   - Reproduce the crash in a test environment.

2. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
   - Analyse server logs, database logs and stack traces with developers.
   - Check for causes such as memory leaks, unhandled exceptions, database
     deadlocks, timeouts, poor concurrency handling, network failures or
     third-party payment gateway issues.

3. REVIEW REQUIREMENTS, DESIGN AND CODE
   - Review the transaction-related requirements and design for missing
     scenarios.
   - Conduct code reviews and static analysis of the transaction module.

4. TESTING ACTIVITIES
   - Functional testing of all transaction flows (transfer, payment, bill
     pay, refund).
   - Negative and boundary testing (invalid amount, insufficient balance,
     session expiry, duplicate submission).
   - Performance, load and stress testing to simulate thousands of
     concurrent users.
   - Endurance (soak) testing to find memory leaks.
   - Failover and recovery testing (what happens if the network or server
     drops in the middle of a transaction).
   - Security testing (authentication, encryption, SQL injection, session
     hijacking).
   - Compatibility testing on different devices, browsers and OS versions.
   - Integration testing with payment gateways and core banking systems.
   - Data integrity testing (transaction atomicity, rollback, no double
     debit).

5. DEFECT MANAGEMENT
   - Log defects in a tracking tool (e.g. JIRA) with priority and severity.
   - Verify fixes with re-testing and run regression tests.

6. PROCESS IMPROVEMENT (QA)
   - Add automated regression and performance test suites in a CI/CD
     pipeline.
   - Define entry and exit criteria for release.
   - Introduce monitoring and alerting in production.
   - Update the test plan and test cases based on lessons learned.

7. RELEASE AND POST-RELEASE
   - Perform smoke and sanity testing after deployment.
   - Monitor production and collect feedback.
   - Track metrics like crash rate, transaction success rate and response
     time.

RESULT: A more stable, secure and reliable application, fewer crashes and
higher customer trust.

Q17. Explain the importance of continuous improvement in Software Quality
     Assurance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Continuous improvement means regularly reviewing and enhancing processes,
practices and products in small, ongoing steps rather than one-time changes.
A common model is the PDCA cycle (Plan - Do - Check - Act). Models like CMMI
and ISO 9001 also stress continual improvement.

IMPORTANCE:
1. Improves quality: Recurring problems are found and removed from the
   process.
2. Reduces defects: Root cause analysis prevents the same defects from
   happening again.
3. Reduces cost: Less rework and fewer late-stage defects.
4. Increases efficiency: Better tools, automation and practices save time.
5. Adapts to change: New technologies, customer needs and market conditions
   require processes to evolve.
6. Increases customer satisfaction: Products keep meeting changing
   expectations.
7. Builds a learning culture: Teams learn from past projects (lessons
   learned, retrospectives).
8. Supports process maturity: Helps an organisation move up the CMMI
   maturity levels.
9. Data-driven decisions: Metrics show what to improve.
10. Competitive advantage: Continuous quality gains make the organisation
    stronger in the market.

Q18. What are the major challenges faced while implementing Software Quality
     Assurance in an organization?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

Major challenges include:

1. Resistance to change: Employees may resist new processes and see SQA as
   extra work or criticism.
2. Lack of management support: Without commitment from top management,
   quality initiatives fail.
3. Limited budget and resources: SQA needs investment in people, tools and
   training.
4. Time pressure: Tight deadlines lead teams to skip reviews and testing.
5. Lack of skilled people: Shortage of trained QA professionals and
   knowledge of standards.
6. Changing requirements: Frequent changes make it difficult to maintain
   plans, test cases and documentation.
7. Poor communication: Gaps between developers, testers, and clients cause
   misunderstandings.
8. Lack of clear standards and processes: Undefined or inconsistent
   procedures.
9. Difficulty in measuring quality: Quality is hard to quantify, and
   selecting proper metrics is challenging.
10. Documentation overhead: Teams may find documentation time-consuming.
11. Tool and technology issues: Selecting, integrating and learning tools
    (automation, CI/CD) is difficult.
12. Cultural issues: A "developers vs testers" attitude, or the belief that
    testing alone is enough.
13. Distributed teams: Coordination in multi-location or outsourced
    projects.
14. Keeping up with new technologies: Cloud, AI, mobile and microservices
    need new testing approaches.

Solutions: Management commitment, training, clear processes, automation,
early involvement of QA, and building a quality-first culture.

Q19. Explain the role of reviews, inspections, and audits in Software
     Quality Assurance.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

These are static quality activities used to detect defects and check
compliance without executing the code.

1. REVIEWS
   - A review is an examination of a work product (requirements, design,
     code, test cases) by team members to find defects and suggest
     improvements.
   - Types: informal review (peer review), walkthrough, technical review.
   - Role: find defects early, share knowledge, improve quality of
     documents and code, and reduce cost of rework.

2. INSPECTIONS
   - An inspection is the most formal type of review, led by a trained
     moderator (Fagan inspection). It has defined roles (moderator, author,
     reader, inspector, recorder), entry and exit criteria, checklists and
     a formal defect log.
   - Role: systematically detect defects, measure the effectiveness of the
     process, ensure that documents and code meet standards, and provide
     data for process improvement.

3. AUDITS
   - An audit is an independent, formal examination of processes and work
     products to check compliance with plans, standards and procedures.
     Audits can be internal or external (by a customer or certifying body).
   - Role: verify that the defined processes are actually followed, find
     non-conformances, give evidence for certification (ISO, CMMI), and
     recommend corrective actions.

SUMMARY: Reviews and inspections mainly focus on the PRODUCT (finding
defects in work products), while audits focus on the PROCESS (checking
compliance). Together they improve quality, reduce defects early and
support continuous improvement.

Q20. Explain how QA activities are performed throughout the Software
     Development Life Cycle (SDLC).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

QA is not done only at the end; it is integrated into every phase of the
SDLC.

1. REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS
   - Review requirements (SRS) for clarity, completeness, consistency and
     testability.
   - Identify ambiguities and missing requirements.
   - Start preparing the test strategy and acceptance criteria.

2. PLANNING
   - Prepare the SQA plan and test plan.
   - Define standards, tools, resources, schedule and risks.

3. DESIGN
   - Review the system design and architecture (design walkthroughs).
   - Check that design meets requirements and standards.
   - Design test cases and test scenarios (using techniques like
     equivalence partitioning and boundary value analysis).

4. DEVELOPMENT / CODING
   - Enforce coding standards.
   - Conduct code reviews, static code analysis and unit testing.
   - Manage version control and configuration.

5. TESTING
   - Execute integration, system, regression and acceptance testing.
   - Report and track defects to closure.
   - Perform performance, security and usability testing.
   - Measure test coverage and defect metrics.

6. DEPLOYMENT / RELEASE
   - Verify release readiness using exit criteria.
   - Perform smoke and sanity testing in production.
   - Check release notes and documentation.

7. MAINTENANCE
   - Regression testing for patches and enhancements.
   - Analyse customer-reported defects and do root cause analysis.
   - Update test cases and processes based on feedback.

Throughout the SDLC, audits, metrics collection, process monitoring and
continuous improvement are also carried out. In models like the V-Model,
each development phase has a matching testing phase.

Q21. Explain with a suitable real-world example how effective Software
     Testing and Quality Assurance can improve customer satisfaction.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ANSWER:

EXAMPLE: An E-commerce application (like Amazon or Flipkart) during a big
sale event.

WITHOUT effective testing and QA:
- The website crashes because of heavy traffic.
- Payments fail or money is deducted twice.
- Wrong prices or discounts are displayed.
- The mobile app hangs on some phones.
- Customers get frustrated, abandon carts, post bad reviews and move to
  competitors. The company loses revenue and reputation.

WITH effective testing and QA:
1. Requirement reviews make sure that all sale-time scenarios (coupons,
   offers, payment options) are clearly defined.
2. Functional testing checks search, cart, checkout, discounts and order
   tracking.
3. Load and stress testing simulate lakhs of users at the same time, so the
   servers are scaled in advance.
4. Security testing protects payment and personal data.
5. Compatibility testing checks that the app works on different devices,
   browsers and OS versions.
6. Regression testing ensures that last-minute changes do not break existing
   features.
7. QA processes (code reviews, CI/CD, monitoring, audits) reduce defects
   from the start.
8. Feedback and defect analysis after the event help in continuous
   improvement.

RESULT:
- The site remains fast and stable during the sale.
- Payments are smooth and secure.
- Fewer complaints and returns.
- Higher customer trust, repeat purchases and positive reviews.
- Increased sales and stronger brand reputation.

Thus effective testing and QA directly improve customer satisfaction and
loyalty.
